3 · Optimized sets

Sixty Upgrades stat weights

The stat-weight scales this sim uses — paste them into Sixty Upgrades (or any Pawn-compatible planner) to rank gear yourself.

How to use these in Sixty Upgrades
  1. Pick the scale that matches your goal (single-target threat, AOE, survival, balanced) and click Copy weights (JSON).
  2. Open your character at sixtyupgrades.com, go to Custom Stat Weights, and paste the JSON. Sixty Upgrades will now rank gear & enchants by it.
  3. Caps come first. Weights don't enforce the hard gates — reach uncrittable (490 Defense, or 5.6% crit reduction) and uncrushable (102.4% combined avoidance with Holy Shield) before maximizing by weight.
How the sim works — the full logic

Everything runs in your browser as a first-principles forward model: final = race/class base + talents + gear + buffs, using real TBC constants — no back-fitting to a sheet. Below is what's under the hood, top to bottom.

1 · The character model

  • Stats: base (level-70 Blood Elf Paladin) + gear (summed item stats, gems & enchants baked in) + flat buffs, then Blessing of Kings ×1.10 on the four primaries (applied after flat buffs like Mark of the Wild's +14). Kings and MotW stack (percentage + flat).
  • Talents are read from your export and applied as modifiers: Anticipation +4 defense/rank, Deflection +1% parry/rank, Toughness +2% item-armor/rank, Sacred Duty +3% / Combat Expertise +2% stamina, Precision +1% hit/rank, Improved Righteous Fury −2% damage taken/rank.
  • Rating → %: defense 2.3654/skill, dodge 18.92/1%, parry 23.65/1%, block 7.88/1%, spell hit 12.62/1%, resilience 39.42 per 1% crit reduction. Defense skill above 350 gives +0.04% each to miss/dodge/parry/block and crit-avoidance.
  • Health: first 20 stamina = 1 HP each, the rest ×10. Armor from items (×Toughness) + 2/agility.

2 · The hard gates (constraints, not weights)

Every set must satisfy these before any stat is maximized — they're pass/fail, not scored:

  • Uncrittable — a level-73 raid boss has +5.6% crit on you; defense (over 350, ×0.04%) + resilience must cover it (≈490 defense, or any defense+resilience mix reaching 5.6%).
  • Uncrushable — miss + dodge + parry + block must total ≥ 102.4% with Holy Shield up (+30% block, or +35.32% with the block libram). AOE Trash drops this gate (level ≤72 mobs can't deal crushing blows).
  • Min HP — a raid-buffed health floor you set per goal (10k = effectively off).

3 · Survival / EHP

  • Physical EHP = health ÷ (1 − armor DR) ÷ (1 − Imp RF damage reduction). Armor DR uses the standard TBC formula (Armor / (Armor + 11960) vs a level-73 boss, capped 75%); the 3/3 Improved Righteous Fury −6% folds in as a flat multiplier.
  • Avoidance is NOT multiplied into EHP. Dodge/parry/miss have diminishing returns — they smooth the average but not the consecutive-hit spikes that actually kill a tank — so EHP is the raw pool behind armor, and avoidance is valued in the weight scales instead.
  • Beyond the uncrush cap, full avoidance beats block. A dodge/parry/miss negates a whole ~5k hit; a block only shaves block-value (~275) off a hit that lands — so once uncrushable the sim values dodge/parry well above block. (Reaching the cap is different: there block chance is prized, because it fills the attack table toward 102.4%.)

4 · Threat

  • Threat is anchored to spell power. Per-ability threat (Consecration, Holy Shield, Avenger's Shield, Judgements, Seals, Retribution/Sanctuary) is modeled from the guide's formulas, all amplified by Righteous Fury ×1.9 and Improved Holy Shield ×1.20.
  • Below the spell-hit cap (17% vs a raid boss) and expertise soft-cap (26), hit/expertise are valued for the missed-threat they recover; at cap they're zeroed.
  • Tier set bonuses are scored as spell-power-equivalents (e.g. Justicar 2pc +10% seal ≈ +19 TPS), so the optimizer values completing a 2pc/4pc — weighed by the goal, so it matters on threat sets and barely registers on survival.

5 · The four sets & the sliders

Caps are gates; the sliders tune how the leftover budget is spent beyond them. Each goal blends an EHP component and a threat component in the ratio you set (e.g. EHP 1 : Threat 4), with its own Min-HP floor. AOE Trash additionally uses AOE-threat weighting and drops the crush gate.

6 · Gems, enchants & metas

  • Per-item socket-bonus worth-it: for each socketed piece the sim compares filling every socket with the globally best gem (forfeit the bonus) vs colour-matching to earn the socket bonus, and keeps whichever scores higher. Gems are tagged by the socket colour to place them in, since the export's socket order is unreliable.
  • Gemming is a lever for the caps: each socketed item enters as a focus variant (goal gems) and a cap variant (avoidance/defense gems), so the optimizer can keep a higher-threat item and def-gem it when that beats a tankier swap. Once uncrushable it stops over-gemming capped avoidance.
  • Meta activation is checked against the whole set's gem colours; a colour-gated meta is enabled by recolouring the cheapest focus sockets, and a final pass verifies every meta still activates after item swaps — an inactive kept meta is flagged and not credited its stats.
  • Enchants are profession- and faction-aware (faction auto-detected from your shoulder inscription); scrolls add flat stats that help meet the gates with less gear.

7 · What you can override

  • Pin any item (the pick or an "≈ also viable" alternate) to force it into a slot and re-optimize the rest around it.
  • Keep gems/enchants to preserve committed pieces across sets; lock trinkets the model can't score (procs/on-use).
  • The stat-weight scales above are the same valuations, exported for Sixty Upgrades — but the sim enforces the caps as gates, which a flat weight list can't.

All values are transcribed from the TBC Prot Paladin guide and are tunable in the source (constants.js, weights.js, threat.js). Caveat: the model can't score proc/on-use trinket effects or unusual fight mechanics — those stay your call.
